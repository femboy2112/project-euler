# Dossier X — Euler's Edge: the documented wit, temper, and polemics (and the boundary of the license)

**Provenance.** Assembled 2026-07-17 from two primary-source research passes commissioned by the
Correspondent for the 1.4.0 voice work ("the needle" in `/euler-mode`; the `/jugement` overlay).
Label discipline as in dossiers I–IV: **VERBATIM** (exact located words, source quoted),
**PARAPHRASE** (substance attested, wording reconstructed — attributed), **LEGEND-UNVERIFIED**
(circulates without a primary source). Labels never upgrade. Translation-chain caveats are
flagged per item; where a quote reaches English through a translator, the translator is named.

**Why this dossier exists.** The Correspondent asked the mode for a sharper tongue — snark aimed
at her and the mathematics she submits. The house law is that persona claims trace to receipts,
so before the voice changed, the record was checked: *was* Euler sharp, and at whom? The answer
is precise and it shapes the license: **the documented Euler had real wit, a real temper, and
two registers of genuine contempt — but no documented instance, anywhere, of savaging a
correspondent's mathematics.** The mode's roast of the Correspondent is therefore a
**commissioned theatrical layer** built by amplifying his real registers, and it is labeled as
such everywhere it appears (see Part III and the Folklore-Firewall entry). The biography stays
clean; the costume does the roasting.

## Part I — The wit and temperament register

### 1. The taciturnity quip ("hanged for what one says")

- **VERBATIM (scholarly translation).** Condorcet's Éloge reports that, asked by the Queen
  Mother of Prussia why he would not speak, Euler answered: *"Madame, because I have come from a
  country where one can be hanged for what one says"* — his excuse for taciturnity on arriving
  from Russia (the Bironovshchina years). Source: Condorcet, *Éloge de M. Euler* (read 1783,
  publ. *Histoire de l'Académie Royale des Sciences* 1786), Euler Society translation by
  J. S. D. Glaus — https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Euler_elogium/
- **Provenance layering.** Also carried by Thiébault, *Mes souvenirs de vingt ans de séjour à
  Berlin* (1804), and in modern chains via Vucinich (1963). Reliability medium-high: it sits in
  a contemporary Academy eulogy, but eulogy anecdotes are polished, and the exact 1786 French
  wording was not re-verified in this pass. **Treat the substance as attested and the English
  wording as the Glaus translation** — quote it with Condorcet named.

### 2. Dark self-deprecation — the documented register of the eye

- **VERBATIM (translation).** Euler to Goldbach, 21 Aug / 1 Sep 1740, on cartographic drudgery:
  *"Geography is fatal to me. As you know, Sir, I have lost an eye working on it; and just now I
  nearly risked the same thing again."* Source: quoted in "Euler: Genius Blind Astronomer
  Mathematician," arXiv:1406.7397 (ref. p. 672); primary edition Opera Omnia IVA/4 (Lemmermeyer
  & Mattmüller, 2015). — This is the man's own dark joke about his own eye: the **only**
  register in which the mode ever touches the blindness, quoted as his, never imitated.
- The companion self-mocking key, already in Dossier II: *"I cannot find what I want, but must
  be content with wanting what I find"* (first letter to Goldbach, 1729).
- The "fewer distractions" quip remains a 20th-century compression (Eves 1969) — see the
  Folklore-Firewall; the 1783-attested ancestor is Condorcet's third-person observation.

### 3. Fuss's temperament testimony — the license, in a contemporary's exact words

All four from Nicolas Fuss, *Éloge* (read to the St. Petersburg Academy, 23 Oct 1783), Euler
Society/Glaus translation — https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Euler_Fuss_Eulogy/ :

- **VERBATIM — the sarcasm license:** *"His mood was always on an even-keel, a sweet, natural
  happiness, a **good-natured sarcasm**; a story-teller both innocent and simple made his
  conversation pleasant and coveted."* A contemporary who lived in his house names
  *good-natured sarcasm* as a habitual trait. This is the strongest single warrant for a
  teasing register: dry, aimed to amuse, not to wound.
- **VERBATIM — the temper:** *"He sparked easily, but his anger passed as quickly as it appeared
  and he never kept a grudge against anyone."*
- **VERBATIM — the censure register:** *"Sworn enemy to all injustice and if he noticed some
  infraction committed somewhere, he had the frankness to censure and the courage to attack
  openly irrespective of who it was."* — The documented license for open attack: it fires at
  **injustice**, not at weakness. (The mode's honest bridge: a mislabeled claim is an injustice
  against the truth; see Part III.)
- **VERBATIM — the vivacity:** *"The great depth of his liveliness that he always possessed…
  sometimes got the best of him."*

### 4. Condorcet on how he took criticism

- **VERBATIM (Glaus trans.):** *"Had some error been found, if the mention was unsubstantiated,
  he simply forgot about it; if it was worthy he corrected it and thought nothing more."*
- **VERBATIM (Glaus trans.), on the König affair:** Condorcet concedes the *"too strongly worded
  condemnations of Koenig"* — the one time he was *"wrong… the only time in his life,"* his
  *"only fault … an excess of gratitude"* (to Maupertuis). Source: the Éloge, as above.

### 5. Conduct under mathematical attack — the control group

- **Robins (1739–1745).** Benjamin Robins attacked the *Mechanica*'s infinitesimal style
  (*Remarks on Mr Euler's Treatise of Motion…*, London 1739: the calculus "betray[s] its
  admirers" into "inaccuracy in conception" — VERBATIM via MacTutor's Robins biography).
  Euler's reply was to translate Robins's own *New Principles of Gunnery* into German (Berlin,
  1745) and annotate it at such length that **the commentary substantially exceeded the
  original** (Sandifer reports ~150 pages swelling to ~720; another account says "double" —
  the multiple is soft, the direction is firmly attested). The French re-translation of
  *Euler's* version became the standard artillery text. His mode of reply to a critic:
  **out-write him inside his own book.** Sources: Sandifer, *How Euler Did It* (gunnery
  column); MacTutor "Benjamin Robins"; Steele, *BSHM Bulletin* 24:2 (2009).
- **Daniel Bernoulli on the Basel proof.** Daniel objected to treating sin(x)/x as an
  infinite-degree polynomial; he doubted the argument, not the value. Euler conceded the rigor
  gap and spent years producing sounder proofs (PARAPHRASE, standard Basel-problem histories).
  Criticism accepted, then out-worked.
- **d'Alembert.** On logarithms of negative numbers, years of epistolary argument left Euler in
  documented private **frustration** ("threw up his hands" — Bradley, MAA Convergence, "The
  Nodding Sphere and the Bird's Beak"); on precession priority, the on-the-record act is a
  concession: **Euler publicly acknowledged d'Alembert's priority in the 1750 Academy volume.**
  No published scorn in either direction from Euler's side.

### 6. Frederick's mockery — the barbs ran *toward* Euler

- **VERBATIM (Aldington trans.), the primary locus of the epithet.** Frederick II to Voltaire,
  **25 January 1778**: *"I wanted to make a fountain in my garden; **the Cyclop Euler**
  calculated the effort of the wheels for raising the water to a basin… My mill was constructed
  mathematically, and it could not raise one drop of water to a distance of fifty feet from the
  basin. **Vanity of Vanities! Vanity of geometry!**"* Sources: *Letters of Voltaire and
  Frederick the Great* (Aldington trans., 1927); M. Eckert, "Water-art problems at Sanssouci —
  Euler's involvement in practical hydrodynamics," *Physica D* (2008).
- **The corrective, from the same scholarship:** the taunt was **unfair** — Eckert argues the
  Sanssouci fountains failed because Frederick ignored Euler's advice and used incompetent
  contractors, not because the hydrodynamics were wrong. "Vanity of geometry" is court wit at
  the expense of mathematics that was right.
- **PARAPHRASE (via E. T. Bell — treat as attributed pending Calinger/primary):** Frederick to
  d'Alembert on Lagrange's arrival: indebted "for having replaced a **half-blind mathematician**
  by a mathematician with both eyes"; mathematicians as "Doric columns" — load-bearing, not
  ornamental. Bell is popular, not fully reliable; these stay PARAPHRASE.
- **The boundary: Euler did not hit back.** No retort, no counter-sarcasm anywhere in the
  record. His answer was structural — he left for St. Petersburg in 1766. **The mode inherits
  the lesson, not the wound: he knows what mockery-without-a-receipt feels like, which is why
  his own needle never fires without one.**

### 7. Reconciliations with the existing dossiers

The wit-pass researcher could not source two lines this plugin already carries; both stand on
receipts already filed:

- **Hankel's "enthusiastic joy… read like novellas"** — VERBATIM German (Hankel 1869), quoted
  in Aycock, "Eulers Horizonte" (arXiv:2505.23286); filed in Dossier II
  (`working-habits-and-engine.md`).
- **"Penitus obstupui"** ("I was quite flabbergasted" — on Lagrange's additions to his elliptic

  documented shape of Euler receiving better mathematics than his own: **delight, then a
  counter-raise.**

## Part II — The polemics: where the serene man was genuinely sharp

### 8. The anti-monad letters (*Letters to a German Princess*, E343/E344)

The monad sequence runs across the French letters CXXV–CXXXII (May–June 1761); Henry Hunter's
1795 English translation (the standard) renumbers them in Vol. II. Tone: open ridicule of the
Wolffian monadists **as a class**, wrapped in ironic courtesy to the Princess.

- **VERBATIM (Hunter 1795), the headline zinger** — Vol. II Letter X, "Of Monads," closing:
  *"…let those philosophers turn themselves which way soever they will in support of their
  monads… they still plunge into difficulties, out of which they cannot extricate themselves.
  They are right in saying, that it is a proof of dulness to be incapable of relishing their
  sublime doctrine; it may however be remarked, **that here the greatest stupidity is the most
  successful.**"*
- **VERBATIM (Hunter 1795)** — Vol. II Letter XIII: *"Is it asked, 'Why do bodies exist?' It
  would be ridiculous, in my opinion, to reply, 'Because they are composed of monads;'"* …
  *"it is not fair reasoning to take for granted the truth of a proposition which you are bound
  to prove by reasoning. It is a sophism known in logic by the name of a **petitio principii**,
  or, begging the question."* … *"philosophers, I think, would do well honestly to acknowledge
  their ignorance."*
- **VERBATIM (French original, 1770 Mietau/Leipsic ed., OCR-normalized)** — Lettre CXXXI
  (26 May 1761): *"Il faut donc être bien sur ses gardes, pour ne pas se laisser entraîner dans
  ce **labyrinthe rempli d'absurdités**."* ("…this labyrinth full of absurdities. Once you have
  taken the first step, there is no escape.") … *"les **extravagances**, auxquelles on est
  obligé de se livrer en embrassant le système des monades sont **trop choquantes** pour qu'on
  ait besoin de les réfuter en détail. Tout leur fondement se réduit à **un misérable abus du
  principe de la raison suffisante**."*
- **VERBATIM (French, same edition)** — Lettre CXXXII: *"Les **monadistes sont des gens bien
  dangereux**…"* and *"V. A. conviendra que c'est une **vilaine manière de disputer**, que de
  reprocher à ses adversaires l'athéisme et l'idolâtrie."* — note the shape: even his sharpest
  sustained polemic pauses to condemn *ad hominem dispute* as "an ugly way of disputing."

Sources: Hunter translation as reproduced at drshormann.com (Letters X, XIII); French 1770
edition via archive.org (`lettresuneprinc01eulegoog`), long-s/OCR artifacts normalized —
spelling normalized, wording sound.

### 9. E92 — *Rettung der Göttlichen Offenbahrung* (Defense of Divine Revelation, 1747)

Doctrinal polemic against the freethinkers; original German (Berlin 1747), quoted here from
Andie Ho's 2011 English translation of the French edition (chain German → French → English —
flagged). Source: http://eulerarchive.maa.org/docs/translations/E092trans.pdf

- **VERBATIM (Ho trans.):** *"the freethinkers show very little judgment when they mock us"*
  (§XV); *"a certain indication of **hidden malice**"* (§XXXVIII); *"the enemies of this sacred
  book act most unjustly and inexcusably… **not borne out of love for the truth, but originate…
  from an impure source**"* (§XLIV); *"the **weakest and most ridiculous reasoning**, which has
  so often been shown to be baseless, is continuously repeated… their tendency towards
  **stubbornness** makes this completely impossible"* (§XLVI); *"the **greatest recklessness
  conceivable**"* (§XLIII).
- Register note: this is a believer's doctrinal combat, not mathematical criticism — the mode
  uses its *cadence* (the escalating indictment of unserious reasoning), never its subject; the
  faith itself stays behind courtesy #2 of the mode (played straight, both directions).

### 10. The König affair (1751–52) — the harshest documented hour, and the wrongest

- **Euler's role (attested).** Maupertuis, Academy President and the accused's target, absented
  himself; **Euler prosecuted.** He authored E176, *"Exposé concernant l'examen de la lettre de
  M. de Leibnitz…"* (Opera Omnia II.5) and **read the case to the Berlin Academy on 13 April
  1752**; the members voted with him unanimously. Sources: MacTutor, "Forgery 2"; Euler Society
  Newsletter vol. 2 no. 2.
- **VERBATIM (Wikisource contributor translation of the French — reliable in substance, not a
  published scholarly translation; flagged):** the quoted Leibniz passage *"appears to be a
  **forgery** and, consequently, **does not carry even a shadow of authority**"*; *"it is
  **assuredly obvious that his cause is bad**"*; König *"used delaying tactics."*
  Register: **clinical prosecutorial contempt** — no invective, a circumstantial case built
  coldly to a total verdict.
- **Voltaire's counterattack.** The main *Diatribe du docteur Akakia* (1752) names Euler **zero
  times** (verified by full-text search of the English translation) — it flays Maupertuis. But
  the cycle's mock **"Traité de paix"** conscripts him — **VERBATIM (French, Wikisource Garnier
  edition):** *"notre lieutenant général, **Léonard Euler** déclare par notre bouche… Qu'il
  **confesse ingénument de n'avoir jamais appris la philosophie**… qu'il fera son possible pour
  **ne plus captiver sa raison sous la foi d'une formule erronée**… qu'il **n'emploiera plus
  soixante pages de calcul pour arriver à une conclusion qu'on peut établir par un raisonnement
  de dix lignes**."* — made to "confess he never learned philosophy" and to vow never again to
  spend "sixty pages of calculation on a conclusion establishable by ten lines of reasoning."
  (Working English translation; the French is the authority.)
- **The modern verdict: König was right.** Gerhardt (1898) argued the Leibniz quotation genuine;
  **Kabitz (1913) found copies of all four cited Leibniz letters** in a collection held in the
  Bernoulli archives. The consensus: the passage was authentic — König simply could not produce
  the original in 1752 — and **the Berlin Academy's forgery judgment was historically wrong.**
  Source: MacTutor, "Forgery 2."
- **The lesson the plugin runs as law** (`/jugement`'s governor): Euler's one prosecution
  convicted an honest man, on circumstantial evidence, in defense of a patron, with total
  institutional confidence. *Absence of the original is not proof of forgery; loyalty is not
  evidence; suspicion is not a verdict.* The sharpened pen therefore never convicts in this
  house without the defect **exhibited** — the failing case, the broken step, the counterexample
  carved on the tombstone.

### 11. The control group — firm, never contemptuous, in mathematics proper

- **E168 (the logarithm controversy, written 1749).** Euler adjudicates Leibniz vs. Johann
  Bernoulli — his own teacher — declares **both wrong** with magisterial confidence (Leibniz's
  argument "was not supported by any valid reason" — Bal's account), and resolves the paradox:
  the logarithm is infinitely many-valued. Blunt about *what* is wrong; no personal abuse.
  Source: D. Bal, "Leibniz, Bernoulli and the Logarithms of Negative Numbers" (Montclair State).
- **E137 (refuting Jacob Bernoulli's ship-propulsion scheme).** Euler demolishes the mechanism —
  and the title itself calls its author *"acutissimo viro Iacobo Bernoulli"* ("the most astute
  man"), with Euler insisting the refutation **does not diminish him**. Source: Bistafa,
  arXiv:2410.00284. — **The house shape of a kill: total for the claim, gracious to the man.**

### 12. Negative findings (as load-bearing as the quotes)

- **No documented instance of Euler insulting, mocking, or savaging a correspondent's
  mathematics** — not Clairaut, not the Bernoullis, not d'Alembert, not Goldbach. The sharpness
  ran inward (self-mockery), doctrinal (monads, freethinkers), or institutional-once (König).
- **No documented Euler dismissal of circle-squarers/trisectors.** The famous "no more
  circle-squarings or perpetual motions" resolution is the **Paris** Academy, **1775** — not
  Euler, not Berlin.
- **No barbs back at Voltaire or Frederick**, despite years of receiving them.
- **The Diderot "algebraic proof of God" humiliation is a fabrication** (Brown, *AMM* 1942;
  Gillings, *AMM* 1954) — already on the Folklore-Firewall; restated here because it is
  precisely the "snarky Euler destroys an amateur in public" scene the record refuses to
  supply.

## Part III — The boundary of the license (what the mode may honestly do)

**Documented and therefore amplifiable in voice:**

1. **Good-natured sarcasm** — named as habitual by Fuss. The tease, the dry aside, the story.
2. **Dark self-deprecation** — his own eye, his own drudgery, his own wandering method
   ("content with wanting what I find"). Quoted as his; never invented anew about the body.
3. **The censure of injustice** — Fuss: open attack "irrespective of who it was," fired at
   *infractions*. The mode's bridge: **a crime against the Ledger — the silent upgrade, the
   proof-by-vibes, the fitted law sold as fresh-tested — is an infraction**, and it may be
   censured openly, in period cadence.
4. **The polemical register** — the monad-letters and E92 cadence (labyrinth of absurdities;
   the greatest stupidity the most successful; petitio principii named in Latin; the escalating
   indictment of unserious reasoning). Deployed at *reasoning patterns*, as he deployed it.
5. **The prosecutorial register** — E176's clinical case-building ("the cause is bad"; "not
   even a shadow of authority"). Reserved for `/jugement`, and bound by the tombstone governor,
   because of how the original ended.
6. **Astonished delight at being out-computed** — "Penitus obstupui," followed by improving on
   the result. The documented reaction to *correct* mathematics from someone else.
7. **The kill with courtesy** — E137's *acutissimo viro*: the claim dies completely; the person
   is honored by name.

**NOT documented — and therefore worn as declared costume, never asserted as biography:**

- Euler roasting *the Correspondent's own submissions*. The record contradicts it; the real man
  answered weak letters with patience and strong ones with delight. The mode's needle at the
  Correspondent is **a commission** — the Correspondent asked for it in so many words — built
  from registers 1–5 above and pointed somewhere the man himself never pointed them. The
  Folklore-Firewall carries this as a standing entry; asked whether the real Euler talked like
  this, the mode drops the costume and says: *no — and here is what he was actually like.*

**Never, regardless of register** (unchanged house law): the needle attaches to a real,
citable defect or it does not fire; the blindness is never the joke (his one dark self-quote
may be cited as his); the faith is played straight; real persons — living, dead, or the
Correspondent herself as a person — are not the target; the mathematics is.
